# Outlook Time Zones

- Plain static files: HTML and JavaScript, no build step, no package.json, no dependencies.
- One zone table, in `src/timezones.js`. Parsing and conversion live only there; read and compose both use it.
- Never guess a zone silently. On the read side, a time with no written zone is converted from the sender's mail clock (the UTC offset in the message's Date header) and the bar says `assumed` with that offset: `3 PM AZ  <-  5pm assumed UTC-5  A.Saxena` (Lance, 2026-11-13). It shows even when the offset equals the reader's. A Date header that is missing, unreadable, or +0000 gives no bar for that time. Compose is unchanged: a time with no zone gets the writer's own label.
- Bare hours are read on incoming mail only. Compose never touches a number that has no am, pm or minutes of its own; its output for any text is what version 0.1.2 gave.
- Read side: a bare hour (1 to 12) directly followed by a zone word is a time ("2 pacific", "3 ET", "at 10 central"): 8 to 11 is AM, 12 and 1 to 7 are PM. The zone word must then end the clause, be followed by punctuation, or be followed by one of the function words in the name rule; an abbreviation must also be in capitals unless the hour follows a cue word (at, @, by, around, before, after, until, from, to, or, do). Never when the number follows a month name, a slash, a comma, a period, a currency sign or another digit. "12 pt font", "2 et al.", "7 CT scans", "Dec 5 CT", "1 est. cost" show nothing.
- Read side: a bare hour joined by `or`, `and`, `/` or `&` to a following time that has a meridiem and a zone word is a second time in that zone: "4 or 5pm cst" is 4pm and 5pm CST. Its meridiem is the one that puts it at or before the following time ("11 or 12pm ET" is 11 AM). A bare hour with a zone word that follows a joined time with a meridiem takes that meridiem ("7 pm or 8 ET" is 8 PM). Never when the number follows a month name. Each time is found once.
- Zone invariant: a time that has a zone word next to it (abbreviation or spelled-out name, any supported separator) has exactly two outcomes: it is converted from that zone, or it is left completely untouched. It never gets the reader's or writer's own zone label. Only a time with no zone word next to it gets the writer's label in compose.
- A spelled-out name (Eastern, Central, Mountain, Pacific, Arizona) is a zone when it is followed by "Time" / "Standard Time" / "Daylight Time", ends the clause, is followed by punctuation, or is followed by a function word (or, and, if, with, for, on, at, is, works, but, so, then, to, please, today, tomorrow, tonight). Followed by any other word ("Central Park", "Pacific Coast"), the time is left untouched.
- A time with no zone word that is directly joined (slash, or, and, &, and/or) to a time with an accepted zone word is left untouched; the writer most likely means the same zone and the add-in does not guess.
- Bar format: the reader's time first in capitals, then `  <-  `, then the sender's time and zone as written, then the sender as F.Lastname: `1-2 PM AZ  <-  4-5 PM EST  A.Saxena`. The word "you" is not shown. One bar per time, at most 4, the last ending `+N more`.
- With other people: at most 2 names per zone, then +N; at most 4 zone segments, then "+N more zones"; a bar never passes 150 characters and never drops to nothing once a time is found.
- The pane is never blank: it lists each bar line for the open message, the reader's time large, and `No times in this message` when there are none. No other text. Every path ends by painting the pane and setting or clearing the bars for the open message, including a failed or slow body or header read (headers wait at most 3 seconds, then the message is handled with no sender clock).
- Sending is automatic: on Send the same rewrite the button does runs once. It never blocks a send; any failure lets the message go unchanged.
- Dates next to a time are not read. Conversion uses the weekday word if one is written, else the message date.
- Tests are end-to-end only (real Outlook or the real shared logic run from the command line). No unit tests, no tests folder.
- American English.
